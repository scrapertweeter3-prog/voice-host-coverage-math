168 hours is a headcount before it is a schedule

An AI voice host that stays on the air around the clock looks like a hiring problem. The unit underneath it is the week itself: 168 hours, each one either covered or dark. Staffing that coverage with people means buying it in 40-hour blocks and hoping the blocks tile the week.

They do not tile. 168 divided by 40 is 4.2, and a payroll cannot hire the 0.2. Four people cover 160 hours and leave the Sunday-night shift dark; the fifth gets hired for what is really an 8-hour slot. Either way the calendar wins the negotiation, because the show needs a voice at 3am as much as at noon.

Priced at the loaded benchmark, coverage comes down to arithmetic. A senior developer runs about $18,290 a month fully loaded, so a 4.2-person coverage roster is $76,818 a month, $921,816 across a year. The 24/7 AI voice-clone host Poket Dev shipped for Mario Nawfal's X Spaces show runs the same 168 hours on the $5,995 monthly plan. The gap is $70,823 a month, and the extra money buys no extra hours of coverage.

Once the deliverable is an always-on system, the shift math stops applying, and the 3am slot costs the same as the noon one.

The same math prices any always-on job. Divide the week by the shift: 168 over 40 gives 4.2 positions before a single task is scoped, and monitoring or support desks pay the same calendar tax. What makes 24/7 expensive is that one job becomes 4.2.
